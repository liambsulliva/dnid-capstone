# Nagging

<NaggingModal />
<ClippyAssistantDemo />

"It looks like you're writing a letter. Would you like help?"

Clippy is a funny example here, but it hints at a larger problem. Capital interests don't always align with user interests. As mentioned in the [Confirmshaming](../coercion/confirmshaming) section, companies will often push features that are in their best interest, even if they are not in the best interest of the user.

This is how you get pop-ups. They suck, they're annoying, and they're intrusive, but they _do_ capture your attention. Let's say you're serving your site to 100,000 users. Capturing the interest of even 1% of your users gives you 1,000 users that are interested in your product. That's a lot of potential revenue sitting on the table.

Of course, this can be a double-edged sword. If you go too heavy on the nagging, you'll alienate users and force them to leave the site. It's a delicate balance. Annoying your users just enough that they will sit through their grievances to access the content they came for.
