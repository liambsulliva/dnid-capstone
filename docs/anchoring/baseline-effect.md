# Baseline Effect

The baseline effect is a cognitive bias that lead people to judge new information based on what they've already seen.

If you're used to Instagram loading in 2 seconds on your phone, you'll be annoyed to see it take 3 seconds on your next visit. But, if it typically takes 5 seconds, you'll be pleasantly surprised to see it load in those 2.

This is especially relevant in the land of e-commerce, where websites will liberally abuse discounted prices to make their products seem more appealing. If you see an item originally priced at $100 selling for $60, it'll seem like a bargain. But, if you see the same item selling at $60 with no baseline price, it won't look nearly as appealing, even though the actual price hasn't changed.

Companies will routinely jack up their baseline price to set the "discounted" price to their original [MSRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturer%27s_suggested_retail_price). Deceptive as it is, it's a powerful way to leave the user feeling that they've discovered a great deal.
